# The agent on the seed cases, 2026-11-16

The agent (`agent-haiku`, `agent-opus`) next to the fixed pipeline it is built
on, on the 194 cases generated from the two seed films. The agent runs the same
routine steps as the pipeline and then lets a model settle what their draft
plan leaves open, so the difference between the two is what the model adds.
Claude Haiku 5.5 was run three times per case and Claude Opus 5.5 once, both at
medium effort.

The rows for ffsubsync 0.5.1, alass 2.0.0 and the untouched input are from the
run of 2026-11-15 on the same cases. Those tools and the cases have not changed
since.

The same caution applies as for the pipeline: two short films, 76 and 26
dialogue lines, and the instructions and safeguards of the agent were worked
out on these same cases. The figures are a first reading and not a held-out
result.

## What stands out

- The agent syncs 94% of the files on Haiku 5.5 and 95% on Opus 5.5. The fixed
  pipeline syncs 87% and the best existing tool 43%. At the wider 500 ms
  tolerance it is 98% for both models, against 89% and 45%.
- Every case against a reference subtitle is solved now, and every case against
  a subtitle plus audio: 100%, up from 91% and 92%. What the pipeline missed
  there were blocks of two to four lines between two cuts. The model places
  them by reading the lines next to the stretch of the reference they can
  belong to.
- Every decision is right: 100%, up from 66%. Most of that is the spoken outro
  after the last line of a film. Against a subtitle alone the rules cannot tell
  whether it is missing from the reference or only unmatched, so the pipeline
  keeps it. The model reads it and removes it.
- No case got worse. On every one of the 194 cases, in every run, the agent
  does at least as well as the pipeline: on success, on the decisions, and on
  the share of lines within 250 and within 500 ms.
- The two models are nearly level. Opus 5.5 syncs one file more than Haiku 5.5
  (180 against 179 of the 190 files that have a right timing) and costs 25
  times as much. Haiku 5.5 is the default.
- The pipeline itself moved from 86% to 87%. A part of a file that is in sync
  is now left alone when another part is not, and against audio its small lead
  or lag is taken as the style of the subtitle and kept for the other parts.

## What is left

All ten or eleven files that still miss have blocks and are synced against
audio alone. Seven or eight of them are within 500 ms. Three are not, on
either model.

- Timing style. Hand-timed subtitles sit about a quarter of a second off the
  voice, which is the edge of the strict tolerance. A file with one offset can
  take its style from all of its lines. A block of three lines cannot.
- A place in a transcript is timed loosely. A subtitle line begins somewhere in
  a transcribed utterance, and the start of that clause is known to about half
  a second. A block that rests on lines the model paired, and on nothing the
  routine steps measured, is placed that loosely.
- A transcript can date a stretch wrongly. In Tears of Steel ten seconds of
  dialogue over music come back eight to twelve seconds off, followed by a
  line the speech model invented five times. The words there are right and the
  times are not. Pairs on such places are taken back, so they do no harm, but
  the lines stay where the draft had them.

Answering every open question with the true pairs, through the same tools,
syncs 184 of the 194 files. That was measured once with a throwaway script and
is not a system in this repository. Opus 5.5 reaches the same 184 and Haiku 5.5
is at 183, so what is left is not for a better model to gain. It is in the
reference and in what the tools make of a pair.

## What the model is asked, and what it costs

- The model is called for 61 of the 194 files, 31%. For the other 133 the draft
  stands on its own and the job costs what the pipeline costs.
- The questions on those 61 files: lines after the last placed line on 49,
  lines between two blocks on 17, lines before the first placed line on 10, and
  a block that rests on little on 3. A file can have several.
- On Haiku 5.5 a file the model is asked about takes 2.1 turns, 6 seconds and
  0.08 cents. Over all files that is 0.03 cents per file, and 5 cents for one
  run of the whole benchmark.
- On Opus 5.5 it takes 2.0 turns, 6 seconds and 2.0 cents. Over all files that
  is 0.6 cents per file, and 1.21 USD for the run.
- A job sends 10,000 to 11,500 input tokens to the model, all but a handful of
  them read from or written to the prompt cache: the instructions and the tool
  definitions are the same for every job. Haiku 5.5 writes about 950 output
  tokens per job, Opus 5.5 about 450.
- Most jobs are two turns: one that pairs lines or settles the lines at an
  edge, and one that submits. Haiku 5.5 reads further lines of the subtitle or
  the reference in about a quarter of its jobs, Opus 5.5 in one of twelve.
- Nothing ran out of budget, no request was declined, no plan of the model was
  kept back, and the fallback model was never used. One tool call in all runs
  was answered with an error, which the model corrected in its next turn.
- The three Haiku runs agree on success for every case, and on the outcome for
  all but one. That file, a Japanese subtitle against audio, comes out right
  in all three runs, but its middle block rests on five of twelve lines, and
  it is reported as synced once and as partial twice. The median error of a file differs by
  6 ms at most between runs.

## What changed after the first full run

The first full run had one line worse than the pipeline had it. In a file
synced against a subtitle plus audio, the model paired a line with the right
line of the reference subtitle, and the line moved by 800 ms: the two subtitles
time that one line differently. One exact pair whose block would differ from
the block next to it by 1.5 seconds or less now only says which block the line
belongs to. Both models were run again after that change, and the figures here
are from those runs. The totals did not move.

## What these runs do not show

- Whether both sides are the same content was never an open question. The four
  wrong-content cases are another film, and the rules reject them without
  doubt. That the model's verdict is taken only where the rules were in doubt
  is covered by tests with a scripted model, and by nothing measured.
- No model chose another fit, and no case had a draft with no timing at all.
  The second was tried once by hand on a made-up file, which Haiku 5.5 solved
  in two turns.
- Sonnet 5.5 can be run as `agent-sonnet` and was not. Other effort levels were
  not compared.
- The seed films are short. A long file sends a longer dossier only where more
  is open, but that is an expectation and not a measurement.
- Japanese audio is untested, as before: both films are spoken in English.

## How it was run

```bash
retimer bench run --systems pipeline,agent-opus --workers 6
retimer bench run --systems agent-haiku --repeats 3 --workers 6
```

Both need `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and `ANTHROPIC_RETIMER_API_KEY`. The transcripts
and embeddings came from the cache, so the time and cost columns show only
what the model added.

File success is the share of files with at least 95% of dialogue lines within 250 ms of their true start, and the column after it is the same with 500 ms. Error columns are medians over files. The tolerance columns pool all dialogue lines. A tool that reports no outcome counts as having left a file alone when no line moved more than 100 ms.
