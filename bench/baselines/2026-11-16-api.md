# The service on the seed cases, 2026-11-16

The 194 seed cases, run through a live service (`api`) as a client reaches it:
every case is uploaded as a job with its audio or reference subtitle, waited
for, and its file and plan are fetched and the job deleted. The server ran on
the same machine with the real providers behind it and Claude Haiku 5.5 as the
agent. Next to it is the agent called directly (`agent-haiku`) on the
transcripts and embeddings the service had just made, so the two rows differ
only in the service around the work.

## What stands out

- The service gives the same answers as the agent called directly. On all 194
  cases the two agree on whether the file is synced, on the median error, and
  on the share of lines within 100, 250 and 500 ms. One file is reported as
  `partial` through the service and as `synced` directly, with the same
  timing: its middle block rests on few lines, and that label already differed
  between repeated runs of the agent.
- Every file the service delivered is the file its plan describes. The runner
  applies the fetched plan to the target on its own side and compares the
  result with the fetched file, byte for byte: 190 of 190. The four
  wrong-content cases ended as `rejected_no_match` and delivered no file.
- No job failed and no request was refused, in 195 jobs and 1,503 requests.
- It syncs 93% of the files, 97% at 500 ms. The agent's baseline of the same
  day says 94% and 98%. The difference is not the service: this run
  transcribed both films anew, and the direct run on these new transcripts
  lands on the same 93%. Thirteen files miss instead of ten or eleven, all of
  them blocks against audio alone, and five of them at 500 ms instead of
  three. So two transcripts of the same audio differ by two or three of these
  files. Cases against a subtitle, or against a subtitle plus audio, are at
  100% in every run.
- All 195 jobs together cost 7 cents at the providers: about 2 cents for
  transcribing the two films once (15 and 12 minutes) and for the embeddings,
  and 5 cents for the model on the 61 jobs that reached it.

## Time

A job runs for a tenth of a second when its transcript is cached and the draft
leaves nothing open, for about five seconds when the model is asked (24 at
the most, with five turns), and for 22 seconds when a 15-minute film has to be
transcribed first. The mean time in the table is longer than that, because it
is what the client waited: the benchmark submits two cases at a time, the
server ran one job at a time, and the runner asks for the status every three
seconds once a job takes long.

## What this run does not show

- The service was reached over plain HTTP on the same machine, as a
  development server. That it refuses plain HTTP in production, and whom it
  believes about HTTPS, is covered by tests against a real server process and
  not by this run.
- One client, one key and two cases at a time. Nothing here says how the
  service behaves under load.
- Both films are short, and their audio is 2.7 and 2.1 MB. A two-hour film
  was not uploaded.

## How it was run

```bash
retimer api-key --label al
RETIMER_API_KEYS=… RETIMER_DATA_DIR=… RETIMER_JOBS_PER_HOUR=2000 RETIMER_REQUESTS_PER_MINUTE=5000 \
  retimer serve --dev --port 8765

RETIMER_API_URL=http://127.0.0.1:8765 RETIMER_API_KEY=… retimer bench run --systems api
RETIMER_CACHE_DIR=<the server's cache> retimer bench run --systems agent-haiku --workers 4
```

The two limits are raised because one key submits 194 jobs within a few
minutes here. The server needs `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and
`ANTHROPIC_RETIMER_API_KEY`.

File success is the share of files with at least 95% of dialogue lines within 250 ms of their true start, and the column after it is the same with 500 ms. Error columns are medians over files. The tolerance columns pool all dialogue lines.
